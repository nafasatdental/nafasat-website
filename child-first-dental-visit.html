When should my child first see a dentist?

A child should have a first dental check-up when the first tooth appears, or by the first birthday at the latest. Early visits are short and gentle, catch decay while it is small, and help children feel at home with the dentist before they ever need treatment.

Before the visit

How to prepare your child

  • Keep the words simple and positive: the dentist will count your teeth.
  • Avoid words like hurt, pain or injection.
  • Book an evening time when your child is rested and fed.
  • Bring a favourite toy, and let them watch you sit in the chair first.
  • Tell us beforehand if your child is anxious, so we plan extra time.

Step by step

What happens at a child's first visit

Written for patients of Nafasat Dental & Aesthetics, NFC Phase 1, Lahore. Every mouth is different, so a check-up always comes first.

  1. Hello first

    Your child meets the dentist and looks around the room. Nothing is rushed.

  2. Counting teeth

    A gentle look at the teeth and gums, often with your child on your lap.

  3. Advice for parents

    Brushing, toothpaste amount, bottles, sweets and thumb sucking, in plain words.

  4. Protection

    Fluoride varnish or sealants if your child needs them.

  5. A Brave Smile Award

    Every child who has treatment goes home with a small gift and a certificate.

Good to know

Your child's first dental visit, answered

Anything else, just ask on WhatsApp. A real person replies.

How much toothpaste should my child use?

A smear the size of a grain of rice under three years, and a pea sized amount from three to six, with fluoride toothpaste.

Do milk teeth really matter if they fall out anyway?

Yes. Milk teeth hold space for adult teeth, help with speech and eating, and decay in them can hurt and spread.

How often should children see the dentist?

Usually every six months, or as the dentist advises for your child.

My child is scared of the dentist. Can you help?

Yes. We go at your child's pace, explain everything in simple words and never force treatment. Short, happy visits build trust.

When do children start losing milk teeth?

Most children lose their first tooth around age six, starting with the lower front teeth.
