Is it right for you?

Who it helps

  • A first visit by the first birthday or when the first teeth appear
  • Toothache, or dark spots on milk teeth
  • Fluoride and sealants to prevent decay
  • Nervous children who need a gentle start

Step by step

What happens

You will know each step before it happens, and you can stop and ask at any point.

  1. Meeting the chair

    Your child sits, rides the chair up and down and meets the mirror before anything else.

  2. A gentle check

    We count teeth, look for early decay and check how the bite is growing.

  3. Talking to you

    We explain what we found in plain words and what to do at home.

  4. Treatment at their pace

    If treatment is needed we may split it across visits so it never becomes too much.

  5. A gift and a chart

    Every treated child takes home a small gift and a brushing chart.

Aftercare

  • No food until the numbness wears off. Watch that they do not bite the lip or cheek.
  • Soft food for the rest of the day.
  • Brush twice a day with a pea-size blob, with a grown-up helping.
  • Keep sweets and juice to mealtimes.
  • See you in six months for a happy check-up.

Good to know

  • Numbness lasts a few hours, so watch that your child does not bite the lip or cheek.
  • Mild soreness for a day or two is normal.
  • Treatment may be spread over more than one visit if your child needs a break.
  • A parent or guardian signs consent for any child under 18.

Good to know

Children's dentistry, answered

Anything else, just ask on WhatsApp. A real person replies.

When should my child first see a dentist?

By the first birthday, or when the first teeth appear. An early, happy visit makes every later one easier.

Do milk teeth really need fillings?

Yes. Milk teeth hold space for adult teeth and help children chew and speak, and decay in them can hurt and spread.

What if my child is scared?

We go slowly, explain each step, and stop for breaks whenever needed. Treatment can be split over more than one short visit.

What are fissure sealants?

A thin protective coating painted into the grooves of back teeth, where most children's decay starts.
